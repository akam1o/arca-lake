# 0.2.0 readiness

Tracking document for the second experimental release, 0.2.0. It
records, per item, what was checked, on which commit, with what result
and what is left. The [0.1.0 readiness](0.1.0-readiness.md) tracker is
the record of 0.1.0 and is not evidence for 0.2.0.

0.2.0 is **experimental**: for evaluation and development, not for
production data. Nothing here claims full S3 coverage, support for
platforms other than Linux x86_64, or performance at scale.

Kinds: **Requirement** (must hold for publication), **Limitation**
(known, disclosed, does not block), **Follow-up** (later release).
Status: **Passed**, **Failed**, **Inconclusive** or **Not run**, for the
evidence named in the row only. A row that passed its tests and CI has
not been exercised by the three-host validation or an R-110 run unless
it says so.

Status: **in preparation**. No candidate commit, no tag, no archive.
Main at the time of writing: `bba959f`.

## Known limitations

- R-117 residual: until a cluster upgraded from 0.1.0 is finalized to
  schema 5, shard writes carrying no incarnation or naming no node are
  accepted and counted; the incarnation is not a secret, so one CA or
  trust domain per cluster is still required
  ([risks](../architecture/risks.md), R-117).
- R-119 residual: groups created by 0.1.0 move no member (including the
  repair of a failed one) until `admin group set-failure-domain` records
  their label; groups that already break their domain are reported, not
  moved.
- Scrub: 0.1.0 storage nodes do not report retirement; their records
  kept for reclaim are `unclassified` and counted as scrub errors until
  the nodes are upgraded.
- R-112: the commit queue lowers `SlowDown` on a hot key but raises the
  acknowledged latency (p50 about 450 ms, p99 about 1.7 s with 64
  writers on one key); the on/off control attributes the gain to the
  queue, with a narrow margin for versioned writes (1.34 × against the
  1.3 × target).
- S3: DeleteObjects with no digest at all is accepted (AWS answers 400);
  an empty `Content-MD5` is treated as absent; shards of a partly sent
  body are removed only by reconciliation (RFC-007 §10).
- Storage: a segment that a 0.1.0 node already reported as corrupt
  after a torn write (`corrupt_segments`, "record truncated") stays
  reported at every open until it is compacted; recovery cannot tell it
  from a damaged segment. Its records before the torn bytes are intact
  (#64).
- Unchanged from 0.1.0: R-109 (tiering GET cost), R-111 (small-object
  overhead), RUSTSEC-2026-0543 exception (R-115), no long-duration soak,
  no physical devices ([known limitations](../guide/known-limitations.md)).

## Incidents during the preparation

- 2026-11-23: build output filled the data disk of HOST_B (a real
  ENOSPC, against the test rules). The build directories of that work
  stream were deleted (only its own data); the disk had about 25 GB free
  afterwards. Data of earlier tests on that disk was not touched; its
  retained-record listing is to be checked at the next R-110 preflight.
- 2026-11-23: an all-features build filled the root file system of
  HOST_C to 100 % for a short time (a real ENOSPC, against the test
  rules). Only that stream's own build targets were deleted; about 23 GB
  free afterwards. A build of another stream running at the time may
  have been affected.
- 2026-11-24: the operator's machine lost its network route to the test
  hosts, and the virtualisation host that runs all three VMs rebooted
  them at once (new boot ids on HOST_A, HOST_B and HOST_C). The three VMs
  share one virtualisation host: they are three operating systems, not
  three physical failure domains. The hot-key on/off control campaign
  that was in progress lost run 9 (invalid) and runs 10–30 (not run);
  its processes ended with the reboot (not a guard stop), and no
  arca-lake process was running afterwards. The campaign resumed on the
  freshly booted hosts under a pre-registered amendment (plan §10.2).
